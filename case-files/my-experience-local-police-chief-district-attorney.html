Suffolk County: the Burke assault, the cover-up, and the Spota/McPartland convictions

1 Summary

(a) What the public record establishes. On December 14, 2012, New York State Probation and SCPD officers arrested Christopher Loeb, then a probationer, at his mother's home in Smithtown. They recovered property stolen from vehicles, including items taken from the department-issued SUV of SCPD Chief of Department James Burke [S-01]. At the Fourth Precinct, while Loeb was handcuffed and chained to the floor, Burke punched and kicked him [S-01][S-02]. Burke then led an effort to conceal the assault [S-01]. He pleaded guilty in federal court on February 26, 2016 to a civil-rights violation and conspiracy to obstruct justice. On November 2, 2016 he was sentenced to 46 months in prison [S-01][S-02][S-03][S-04]. On December 17, 2019, a federal jury convicted former Suffolk District Attorney Thomas J. Spota and Christopher McPartland, former chief of the DA's Government Corruption Bureau, on all four counts for their roles in the cover-up [S-09][S-10]. On August 10, 2021, each was sentenced to five years in prison [S-11][S-12][S-13].

(b) Related proceedings. Loeb's federal civil-rights suit, Loeb v. County of Suffolk et al., No. 2:15-cv-00578 (EDNY), was settled. The court approved a $1,500,000 settlement paid by the County and six named officers, and the claims against Burke were dismissed by stipulation later in 2018 [S-14][S-15][S-16]. In January 2017, a state judge vacated Loeb's 2014 state weapons conviction after the special prosecutor agreed the testimony behind it was "based on perjury." The original indictment was reinstated [S-17]. On November 8, 2017, Justice Ambro dismissed the reinstated indictment for good, finding that police misconduct had impaired the integrity of the grand jury proceeding, and barred prosecutors from refiling the charges [S-26][S-27]. Later true-crime coverage, including Investigation Discovery's Unraveled: Long Island Serial Killer podcast and TV special, used Loeb's account as a starting point [S-18][S-19].

(c) Personal experience / opinion. Reserved for Christopher's first-person account, clearly labeled as perspective. Keep it out of the record-facts paragraphs above.

(d) Unadjudicated allegations. This file does not repeat claims that no court has decided. That includes allegations made in true-crime media about Burke and the Gilgo Beach investigation. The only coverage noted is coverage that exists [S-18][S-19].

Key questions this file raises

  • How did the chief of a large police department carry out a cover-up for about three years, with help from the county's top prosecutor? [S-02][S-09]
  • What safeguards failed when, according to federal prosecutors, a detective testified falsely at a state pretrial hearing in October 2013? [S-01]
  • Why did the first federal grand jury investigation (April–December 2013) close, and what led to its reopening in mid-2015? [S-09]
  • What did the County's $1.5M settlement cost taxpayers, and was any of it recovered from the officials responsible? [S-15][S-16]

2 Timeline

Each entry: date, neutral description, and the source ID from the table below.

  1. Dec 14, 2012
    Probation and SCPD officers arrest Loeb at his mother's Smithtown home and recover items stolen from vehicles, including Burke's SCPD SUV (gun belt, ammunition magazines, cigars, and a bag). Burke assaults Loeb at the Fourth Precinct while Loeb is handcuffed and chained to the floor. Source: [S-01][S-02][S-09]

    A federal grand jury investigation into the assault begins. Source: [S-09]

    The federal grand jury investigation is closed. Source: [S-09]
  5. 2014
    Loeb pleads guilty in state court to criminal possession of a weapon and is sentenced to three years. Source: [S-17]
  6. Feb 5, 2015
    Loeb files a civil-rights suit, Loeb v. County of Suffolk et al., 2:15-cv-00578 (EDNY). Source: [S-14]
  7. Mid-2015
    The federal investigation reopens. Source: [S-09]
  8. Dec 8–9, 2015
    Burke is indicted (Dec 8), then arrested and arraigned (Dec 9). Case: United States v. Burke, 15-CR-627 (EDNY). Source: [S-01][S-05]
  9. Feb 26, 2016
    Burke pleads guilty to a civil-rights violation and conspiracy to obstruct justice. Source: [S-01][S-03]
  10. Nov 2, 2016
    Judge Leonard D. Wexler sentences Burke to 46 months in prison and 3 years of supervised release. The government had asked for 51 months. Loeb gives a victim impact statement. Source: [S-02][S-04][S-05][S-06][S-07][S-08]
  11. Nov 7, 2016
    Judgment entered: 46 months, 3 years supervised release, $200 special assessment, no fine and no restitution. Source: [S-03]
  12. Jan 31, 2017 (reported)
    State Judge Richard Ambro vacates Loeb's 2014 conviction, with the special prosecutor's agreement. Loeb is released and the original indictment is reinstated. Source: [S-17]
  13. Nov 8, 2017
    Justice Ambro dismisses the reinstated 2012 indictment (No. 03122A-2012) for good and denies the special prosecutor's request to present the case to another grand jury, barring prosecutors from refiling the charges. Source: [S-26][S-27]
  14. 2017
    Spota and McPartland are charged federally, United States v. Spota et al., 17-CR-587 (EDNY). Source: [S-09]
  15. Feb 7, 2018
    The Suffolk County Attorney tells the court that a $1,500,000 settlement has been approved. Source: [S-15]
  16. Feb 14, 2018
    The court approves the settlement and dismisses the claims against the County defendants with prejudice. Source: [S-14][S-16]
  17. Nov 2018
    Claims against Burke are dismissed by stipulation (Nov 9), and the civil case is closed (Nov 15). Source: [S-14]
  18. Apr 2019
    Burke completes his prison sentence. Source: [S-10]
  19. Dec 17, 2019
    After a trial before Judge Joan M. Azrack, a jury convicts Spota and McPartland on all four counts: conspiracy to tamper with witnesses and obstruct an official proceeding, witness tampering, obstruction of justice, and being accessories after the fact to Burke's deprivation of a prisoner's civil rights. Source: [S-09][S-10]
  20. Dec 11, 2020
    Investigation Discovery's Unraveled: Long Island Serial Killer (Billy Jensen and Alexis Linkletter) is announced, with Loeb's story as its starting thread. Source: [S-18]
  21. Mar 2021
    Rolling Stone covers Unraveled. Episode One features Loeb telling his story, and the TV special premieres March 9 on Discovery+. Source: [S-19]
  22. Aug 10, 2021
    Judge Azrack sentences Spota and McPartland to five years in prison each. Spota is also fined $100,000. Source: [S-11][S-12][S-13]
