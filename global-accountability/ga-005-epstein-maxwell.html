Global Accountability GA-005

Epstein & Maxwell: Trafficking Conviction and the Deal That Failed Victims

1 Summary

On December 29, 2021, a federal jury in Manhattan found Ghislaine Maxwell guilty of sex trafficking of a minor, sex trafficking conspiracy and related charges for her role in a scheme to sexually exploit and abuse multiple minor girls with Jeffrey Epstein [S-01]. On June 28, 2022 she was sentenced to 240 months in prison [S-01][S-02], and on October 6, 2025 the U.S. Supreme Court declined to hear her appeal [S-04]. In November 2020, DOJ's Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) concluded that then-U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta's decision to resolve the 2006–2008 federal Epstein investigation through a non-prosecution agreement "constitutes poor judgment" [S-03]. OPR also concluded he exercised poor judgment by not making sure victims were notified of the state plea hearing [S-03]. OPR found no professional misconduct by any of the five attorneys it investigated, and no evidence that the decision was based on corruption or on Epstein's wealth, status or associations [S-03].

2 Follow the money

Maxwell was ordered to pay a $750,000 fine, in addition to her prison term and five years of supervised release [S-01]. OPR found that the 2007 non-prosecution agreement included provisions to help victims recover monetary damages from Epstein [S-03]. It also noted that Epstein reportedly settled multiple civil suits by mid-2010 and in the following decade [S-03]. The cited sources give no settlement totals [S-01][S-03].
