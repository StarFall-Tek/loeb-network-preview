Global Accountability GA-007

"Kids for Cash": Judges Who Sold Children's Freedom

United States (Luzerne County, Pennsylvania) · a sourced Global Accountability entry built from court records and official findings.

1 Summary

After an 11-day trial in February 2011, a federal jury found former Luzerne County Judge Mark Ciavarella guilty on 12 of 39 counts, including racketeering, racketeering conspiracy, money laundering conspiracy, honest services mail fraud and filing false tax returns [S-01][S-02]. He was acquitted of the remaining counts, including extortion [S-02][S-03]. In August 2011 Senior U.S. District Judge Edwin M. Kosik sentenced him to 336 months (28 years) [S-01][S-02]. On May 24, 2013 the Third Circuit upheld the conviction and sentence, apart from one mail-fraud count dismissed on statute-of-limitations grounds [S-01][S-02]. His co-defendant, former President Judge Michael Conahan, pleaded guilty to racketeering conspiracy in April 2010 [S-01]. The Supreme Court of Pennsylvania vacated thousands of juvenile adjudications because of Ciavarella's conduct as a juvenile court judge [S-01][S-03][S-04].

2 Follow the money

The jury found Ciavarella should forfeit $997,600, the sum he received from Robert Mericle, the developer who built the juvenile detention facilities [S-01][S-02]. The court ordered restitution of $965,930 to the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and $207,861 relating to the tax charges [S-01]. The Third Circuit gives the total restitution as $1,173,791.94 [S-02]. Prosecutors also sought forfeiture of about $2.8 million in assets from both defendants, but that figure is a charge, not a finding [S-01].
