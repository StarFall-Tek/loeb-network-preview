Global Accountability GA-006

Nassar and the FBI: When Federal Agents Sat on Abuse Reports

United States · a sourced Global Accountability entry built from court records and official findings.

Every statement below carries a source ID that links to the Sources table.

Entry info

CountryUnited States
ThemeChild sexual abuse; law-enforcement failure
OutcomeDOJ Inspector General report (Jul 14, 2021) found FBI Indianapolis failed to act with urgency, made fundamental errors, and produced a report with materially false information [S-01][S-02]
StatusOfficial DOJ OIG finding (Report 21-093) [S-01]

1 Summary

The DOJ Office of the Inspector General found that senior officials in the FBI Indianapolis Field Office did not respond to allegations of sexual abuse by former USA Gymnastics physician Lawrence Gerard Nassar with the urgency they required [S-01]. The office made fundamental errors, did not notify the FBI Lansing Resident Agency or state and local authorities, and did not take other steps to reduce the ongoing threat [S-01][S-02]. A supervisory agent did not document a September 2015 victim interview until 17 months later, and the report then written "included materially false information and omitted material information" [S-02]. From July 2015 until September 2016, Nassar continued to treat gymnasts in Michigan [S-01]. The investigations ultimately found he had sexually assaulted over 100 victims, and he was convicted in federal and state court and sentenced to more than 100 years in total [S-01].

2 Follow the money

The cited sources document no bribes, payments, fines or settlement figures [S-01][S-02]. This entry rests on the Inspector General's findings about how the FBI handled the case [S-01][S-02].
