Rotherham: 1,400 Children and the Reports Nobody Read

United Kingdom (England) · a sourced Global Accountability entry built from court records and official findings.

Every statement below carries a source ID that links to the Sources table.

StatusOfficial inquiry finding (Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Exploitation in Rotherham, 1997–2013) [S-01]

1 Summary

An independent inquiry led by Professor Alexis Jay concluded that at least 1,400 children were sexually exploited in Rotherham between 1997 and 2013, and called this a conservative estimate [S-01]. It found that the collective failures of political and officer leadership over the first twelve years of the inquiry period were "blatant", and that senior managers in social care underplayed the scale of the problem [S-01][S-04]. It found the police gave no priority to child sexual exploitation and failed to act on the abuse as a crime [S-01]. Three reports from 2002, 2003 and 2006 were known to the police and council [S-01]. The first was "effectively suppressed" because some senior officers disbelieved its data, and the other two were ignored [S-01][S-02]. The council leader resigned on the day the report was published [S-03].

2 Follow the money

The cited sources document no bribes, payments, fines or settlement figures [S-01][S-02]. This entry rests on the inquiry's findings about institutional failure [S-01].
