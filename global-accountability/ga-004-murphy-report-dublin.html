Global Accountability GA-004

The Murphy Report: Church, Gardaí and the Dublin Cover-Up

Ireland · a sourced Global Accountability entry built from court records and official findings.

Every statement below carries a source ID that links to the Sources table.

Entry info

CountryIreland
ThemeChild sexual abuse; cover-up, including police deference
OutcomeCommission of Investigation report published Nov 29, 2009: complainants met with "denial, arrogance and cover-up" [S-01][S-02]
StatusOfficial statutory finding (Commissions of Investigation Act 2004) [S-01]

1 Summary

The Dublin Archdiocese Commission of Investigation reported on how Church and State authorities handled a representative sample of child sexual abuse allegations against clerics from 1975 to 2004 [S-02]. It found that complainants were "often met with denial, arrogance and cover-up" [S-02]. None of the four Archbishops in the period reported his knowledge of child sexual abuse to the Gardaí in the 1960s, 1970s or 1980s [S-02]. When names were handed over in November 1995, the 17 given were not complete, because the Archdiocese then knew of at least 28 priests with complaints against them [S-02]. The Commission also found that some very senior Gardaí "clearly regarded priests as being outside their remit", and that in some cases Gardaí reported complaints to the Archdiocese instead of investigating them [S-02]. The Minister for Justice published the report on November 29, 2009 [S-01].

2 Follow the money

The Commission found that the Archdiocese first made inquiries about insurance cover for compensation claims in the mid-1980s and put cover in place in 1987 [S-02]. It called taking out that insurance "an act proving knowledge of child sexual abuse as a potential major cost to the Archdiocese" [S-02]. The cited sections give no compensation or settlement totals [S-01][S-02].
